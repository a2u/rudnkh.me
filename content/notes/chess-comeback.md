---
title: "Getting back into chess at 30"
date: 2026-03-25
---

Restarted chess seriously in January after a decade of dabbling. Current
Lichess rapid: 1970. Target: 2200 by year-end.

## What's working

- **Puzzle rush**, 10 minutes a day. No analysis, just pattern drilling.
- **One annotated master game per day.** Slow, notes on paper. I've
  learned more from 40 games this way than from 500 online blitz.
- **Post-mortem every rated loss.** No excuses, no engine — first I
  identify the moment I went wrong, *then* I turn on the engine.

## What isn't

- Blitz. It's fun but it's ruining my calculation discipline. Limiting to
  one session a week.
- Openings obsession. I was building a repertoire like it was a hobby in
  itself. Now I play two openings on each side, and I study positions
  arising from them, not openings in the abstract.

## Books I'm actually reading

1. *My System* — Nimzowitsch. Occasionally dated, structurally
   essential.
2. *Think Like a Grandmaster* — Kotov. The "analysis tree" discipline is
   worth the book on its own.
3. *Pump Up Your Rating* — Axel Smith. Practical, no-nonsense.

The single biggest lesson so far: **sit on your hands**. Almost every
blunder in my loss database comes from a move I could have spent thirty
more seconds on.
